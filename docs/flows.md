# The cell's information flows (ERC-8489: foga flow …)

A cell takes part in two information-flow contexts from the day it is born. It *speaks about itself*: its registration, AID document, descriptor, unit and card are published, re-published when they change, read by anyone, and kept. And it *takes part in a market*: demanders mint ERC-8414 tenders, cells submit, every submission is public on chain, the acceptance authority settles. ERC-8489 (the Agent Information Flow Paradigm, the twelfth covenant and an *extension* in 0.9.0 — docs/extending.md) gives both a registered shape: a **paradigm** (a content-addressed description of the rules of a context — carriers, operators, thirteen rule dimensions, who may acquire which role by which predicate), a **channel** that adopts one paradigm at a time, **role bindings** (who holds DISCOVER, OBSERVE, SUBMIT, RESPOND, REDISTRIBUTE … in which channel, on what basis, since when), **four-valued checks** (SATISFIED, NOT_SATISFIED, UNDETERMINABLE, VERIFIER_ERROR — an unverifiable condition is never recorded as a failed one), **anchors** of what was published and **disclosure records** of what was made observable to whom. Transport is out of scope: MCP, A2A, HTTP and the chain's events carry the messages as before; the registries add the rule and authority layer they can be checked against.

FOGA runs this as `foga flow …` (the catalog's `flow_*` capabilities, `src/tools/flows.ts`), with the cell's part kept by `src/node/flows.ts`, the registries read and written by `src/core/aifp.ts`, FOGA's two paradigms in `scenarios/flows/` (`src/core/paradigms.ts`), and FOGA's own predicate verifier in `contracts/src/FogaIdentityVerifier.sol`. The 0.9.0 slice is **the cell's own channel, the market's admission, the anchors and disclosures of its publications**, each an act through the account, each recoverable, each readable by anyone. The reading of the ERC that FOGA settled on (the design review, 2026-11-09) is in the rulings below.

## Two paradigms, one family

The maintainer of a world registers two paradigms once, in the family `foga` (`foga flow bootstrap`, or `foga world deploy` on a local world, which does it with the deployer's key unless `--no-flows` leaves it to a cell). `foga flow bootstrap` works from the reference cell's configuration and the owner's key: a world file without the registries (a cell born before the covenant) first gains the seven from the packaged world of the chain (or `--from <world.json>`), FOGA's identity verifier is deployed from the bundle when the world lacks it (the upstream deploys only its own contracts), and then the capability `flow_bootstrap` registers the paradigms and opens the market through the cell's account, which becomes the family's controller and the market's authority. Their descriptors ship in the package; the canonical validator's checks (`validateDescriptor`) run before anything is registered, and the ids are `keccak256(JCS(descriptor))`, so a descriptor and its id can be checked by anyone against the bytes the registry emitted.

| Paradigm | What it is | Labels | Roles and their predicates | The dimensions that matter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `foga:cell-source/v1` | the cell's self-description: one publisher, open observation, an archive of what it said about itself | `aifp:paradigm/source/v1`, `aifp:paradigm/archive/v1` | DISCOVER, OBSERVE, REDISTRIBUTE → `open` (anyone may admit itself); SUBMIT → the authority's manager grant (the cell grants itself) | discovery public-directory; delivery pull; history immutable, a late joiner sees all; forward, quote, derive and publish elsewhere allowed; contributors identified; object relations `supersedes`, `retracts`, `references` |
| `foga:tender-market/v1` | the world's public tender market | `aifp:ext/finchip.ai/tender-market/v1` — no reserved label: submissions are public on chain, so it is not an `intake`; the acceptance authority's settlement is not a `select` | OBSERVE → `open`; SUBMIT and RESPOND → `identity` (kind 7): the subject's account owns an ERC-8004 identity in the world's registry and its ERC-8434 anchor is ACTIVE, judged by the world's `FogaIdentityVerifier` | one carrier (delivery), one operator (fan-in), contributors qualified; acquisition by predicate; request-response correlation; the TaskToken's events as the transport — identity, push, history retention, late-join history, total order; **not** delivery records, since an event proves nothing about who received it |

The market's descriptor names the world's identity verifier and ERC-8004 registry, so **its id is particular to the world**; the cell-source descriptor names nothing and is the same on every world (`0xc5a7f453…`). A world file's `flows` block records what its maintainer registered — the paradigm ids, the family's controller, the default market channel and its authority — and a cell's configuration may name another market (`flows.market.channelId`). `foga:direct/v1` (the entrance sessions) is not in 0.9.0: a channel adopts one paradigm at a time, and the entrances are the cell-source channel's transport bindings, declared with the capabilities each one actually has (MCP: identity, pull, stream; A2A: identity, pull; the HTTP face: identity, pull, history, late-join history), never with delivery records.

## What a cell does

Everything below goes through the account's `execute`, so the subject the registries derive from `msg.sender` is the **cell account** — never the operator's key, never an `erc8004:` reference (ERC-8489 section 3). The owner's acts are the structural ones; the operator's are the recurring ones (the permission table below).

1. **At birth** (`foga cell init`, `foga join`; or `foga cell extend --covenants 8489` for a cell that runs already): `openChannel(foga:cell-source/v1, authority = the account, bindings = the entrances)` — the channel id is written into `cell.config.json` (`flows.channelId`) — then `grant(self, SUBMIT)`, because an authority holds no role until it grants one. The channel's metadata (`foga.flow-channel/1`) says what the channel is and where its objects are read.
2. **Every `docs_publish`**: the publication's object is `keccak256(JCS({schema: "foga.publication/1", documents}))` over the manifest's content hashes — the same content is the same object. The first is anchored as `publish`; every later one as `supersede` with a `supersedes` relation to the one before; the same object is never anchored twice, and an object anchored by another subject is not this cell's to anchor. When the cell has a public URL, the publication is also recorded as `DISCLOSED` to the public audience (`ext:foga:public`): a fact that the documents were made readable by anyone, not a right granted and not a proof that anyone read them. Both are steps of the same keyed job as the commitments, so a publish interrupted after the commitments resumes with the same calldata.
3. **The market** (`flow_admit`, run by `foga join` after the documents and by the watcher otherwise): the identity predicate is **previewed** first (`previewAdmission`, a view) with the evidence — the account and its ERC-8004 agent id — and the roles the cell holds already are left alone; on SATISFIED, `admit(market, SUBMIT)` and `admit(market, RESPOND)` go through the account and the bindings are recorded. On any other outcome nothing is sent: the refusal is at the `admission` gate and names the outcome the registry would have obtained — **NOT_SATISFIED** (the condition is shown not to hold: no identity, an identity owned by someone else, an anchor that is not ACTIVE), **UNDETERMINABLE** (the registry cannot answer: another verifier's vocabulary, another registry, no evidence), **VERIFIER_ERROR** (a verifier that did not answer) — beside the cell's current eligibility. A read that fails is a failed call, never an outcome.
4. **Before taking a tender** (optional policy, `policy.tenders.requireCounterpartyFlowRole`): `tender_precheck` evaluates the demander's SUBMIT role in the market (`evaluateNow`) and refuses at the `flow` gate, before the verifier and budget gates, when it is not SATISFIED — the outcome kept as obtained, an unreadable registry reported as a failed call and not as a verdict. **The historical role and the current eligibility are two things**: `evaluateNow` reads the bindings the registry holds (granted or admitted, not revoked, not suspended, not pending re-admission) and re-judges no predicate; whether the counterparty's identity is still owned and its anchor still ACTIVE is read beside it (`flow_status` says both for the cell itself).
5. **Propagation** (`spread-the-cell`): a replication pointer is given only where the source allows redistribution, the actor holds REDISTRIBUTE in the target, and the receiver is willing — three facts, each its own; the event anchor checks the second, the runtime the rest.

**What the node does on its own** (`flows.auto`, on by default): the watcher looks, on its pace (`flows.checkEverySeconds`, 600 s, reads only), for a publication not anchored (the documents of a cell born before the channel, a fact the store lost — what the chain holds already is recorded first, so nothing is anchored twice) and for a market the cell is not admitted to yet, and does one of them a tick through the same capabilities as the caller `watcher`; a cell that is not eligible is not asked for every tick. The tick line gains a `flows` clause when it acted; the journal a `flows-watch` entry.

## Rulings (the design review, 2026-11-09)

- **An extension, not a base covenant**, until the twelve-covenant cell specification (4c): the base set `FOGA_COVENANTS` is unchanged, a world with the registries advertises `ERC-8489` under `world.extensions`, and the doctor says `NotTested` at layer 5 until a scenario exercises it.
- **The public market is not an intake.** Submissions on the TaskToken are public, so `foga:tender-market/v1` carries no reserved label (an `intake` requires collector-only visibility) and no `select` operator (a hashlock settlement is the authority's judgment, not a dispatch); the descriptor states the real dimensions and an `aifp:ext/finchip.ai/…` label.
- **The subject is bound first.** The identity verifier takes evidence naming an account and an agent id, derives the account's `eip155-address` reference and compares it with the subject being admitted before it reads any registry; the two registries it reads are immutables of the deployment, never taken from the caller; a predicate naming another registry is UNDETERMINABLE here, not judged. Tested: the operator's key as the subject, another cell's agent id, a forged registry, an identity transferred away, a STALE anchor, a registry that does not answer (contracts/test).
- **Historical role and current eligibility are checked and recorded separately.** `evaluateNow` is the registry's own four-valued read of what was bound; `currentEligibility` is FOGA's read of the identity and the anchor now; neither stands in for the other, and a failed RPC call is a failed call.
- **Recovery reads before it writes.** `flow_admit` reads the roles the cell holds and admits only the missing ones (the registry caps bindings per subject and channel); the anchors reconcile with `anchoredBy` before planning; the same documents are the same publication object and the same job.
- **The default market is optional and replaceable.** The world's maintainer opens one; a cell joins it (`flows.market.join`), names another (`flows.market.channelId`) or none; the maintainer's authority over the default market is stated as it is (`flow_status`, the manifest), and it is not the only legitimate way to the TaskToken.
- **One authorization path, the account's.** The owner's acts through `execute` (open, grant, bootstrap), the operator's under the `flows` scope group; nothing granted to the operator's address on the registries; a one-key cell holds the owner's pairs for itself alone (operator-scope.ts).
- **Not yet**: the ACDF governance binding of the market (a `decision` executor in confirmed mode — once the "decision verified → execution authorized → result recorded" path is in place), `foga:direct/v1`, re-admission after a rule change (`readmit` is encoded and scoped; no FOGA paradigm has a second version yet), committed subjects.

## The permission table

| Call | Who | Route | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| `registerParadigm` (×2), `openChannel` (the market) | the maintainer (the owner of the reference cell) | `execute` by the owner | a one-key cell's owner pairs |
| `openChannel` (the cell's channel), `grant` (SUBMIT to itself) | the owner | `execute` by the owner | a one-key cell's owner pairs |
| `admit`, `readmit` | the operator | `execute` under the allow-list | `flows` |
| `anchor` | the operator | `execute` under the allow-list | `flows` |
| `recordDisclosure` | the operator | `execute` under the allow-list | `flows` |
| `evaluateNow`, `rolesOf`, `previewAdmission`, `bindingOf`, `channel`, `anchoredBy` … | anyone | reads | — |

A cell born before 0.9.0 gains the `flows` group by `scope_complete` (which `foga cell extend --covenants 8489` runs); `operator_rotate` closes a one-key cell's owner pairs when the roles separate.

## Over MCP and HTTP

`flow_status` and `flow_roles` are public reads (the MCP tool list of the public, `POST /api/flow_roles`): a counterparty asks for the cell's roles in the market, and the cell asks for the counterparty's, before dealing. The acts (`flow_admit`, `flow_anchor`, `flow_disclose`) are the operator's; `flow_open` and `flow_bootstrap` the owner's (admin, CLI only). The manifest (`/.well-known/foga-cell.json`) carries a `flows` block — the registries, the cell's channel and paradigm, the market, where the flow documents are served; the card a `flows` line. The MCP resource `foga://docs/flows` is this page.

## Doctor and preflight

The doctor covers the seven upstream contracts and FOGA's verifier at layers 1–3 (code present, ERC-165 ids against the published ones, runtime code against the locked build — the Sepolia composite verifier is a known revision seam, v0.1.1 against the locked v0.1.2, said so in the world file), checks at layer 4 that the registries point at each other and the verifier at the world's own registries, and reads the clock mode and the leaf gas bound. `foga node preflight` adds a `flows` area: the paradigm registered on the chain, the channel open with the cell as authority, SUBMIT held, the current publication anchored, the market joined (or not asked for), the watcher on.

## Verification

`test/aifp.test.ts` holds `src/core/aifp.ts` to the ERC's published vectors (subject references, predicate encodings, the outcome table, interface ids, the canonical bytes and ids of seventeen example descriptors) and validates FOGA's two. `contracts/test/FogaIdentityVerifier.t.sol` is the identity verifier's matrix. `test/flows.test.ts` is the 4b acceptance on anvil: the deployer's bootstrap, a cell's channel at birth, the publication anchored then superseded and never twice, the admission by the identity predicate with the stranger's NOT_SATISFIED, a verifier without code VERIFIER_ERROR, an unknown kind UNDETERMINABLE and `anyOf` keeping a fault inside its leaf, the `flow` gate refusing a demander without the role and passing once admitted, a cell extended in place whose watcher joins the market and anchors, the served documents, the manifest, the card and the CLI.

One finding for the upstream repository: the ERC's compatibility rules (section 5.4) say a validator MUST reject a descriptor that trips them, and the canonical validator at the locked commit checks none (its own `blind-review` composite carries `anonymous-qualified` visibility with no credential predicate and is published as valid); FOGA reports them as warnings and stays byte-compatible with the registries.
